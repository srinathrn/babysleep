The 5 S's — in order
Swaddle snugly → Side/stomach hold → Shush loudly near ear → Swing with small rapid jiggles → Suck (pacifier or feed). Each step amplifies the next one.
Hold position
Football hold facing down
Lay baby face-down along your forearm, tummy on your arm, head near your elbow. Your palm gently supports the belly. Gentle pressure relieves gas and colic almost instantly.
Sound tip
Sound must match crying volume
The womb is ~85–95 dB — louder than a vacuum cleaner. Your shushing or white noise should match baby's cry volume to compete. Gradually lower it once baby is calm.
Movement
Fast tiny jiggles, not slow rocking
At 3–6 months, babies respond better to small rapid jiggles (1–2 inches, ~1 per second) than slow rocking. Always keep the head supported. Never shake vigorously.
Feeding check
Rule out hunger and gas first
At 3–6 months, feedings are every 3–4 hours. If last feed was 2+ hours ago, try feeding first. For gas relief: bicycle the legs, or massage tummy gently in clockwise circles.
Environment
Dark, warm, and no stimulation
Room at 68–72°F (20–22°C). Blackout dark. Remove toys or mobiles from view. Swaddle arms snugly — the startle reflex frequently wakes babies at this age.
Calming words
What to softly say
"Shhh… you're safe… I've got you… shhh… all is well…" — repeat slowly in a low, monotone voice. Your calm voice signals safety to baby's nervous system and helps regulate them.
Call your doctor if…
Warning signs
Crying lasts 3+ hours, baby has fever, unusually high-pitched cry, refuses to eat, consistently arches back, or crying started suddenly from sleep. Always trust your instincts.
